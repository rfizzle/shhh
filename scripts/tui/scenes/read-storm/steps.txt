# A turn that reads its way into a task before it has anything to say about
# it (docs/interface/surfaces.md#the-step). Twelve reads with no plan and no
# prose over them: the run is one card all the same, counting them on its
# header with a strip of twelve glyphs under it, and the reading cursor opens
# it onto its group of reads in place.
setup for f in one two three four five six seven eight nine ten eleven twelve; do printf 'package fixture\n' > "${f}.go"; done
snap 01-start "Some things worth doing first"
keys "how is the round limit counted" Enter
# Twelve calls, one card: what they were and how many, and the order they
# came in as a strip.
snap 02-folded "read 12 files" "⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙⚙"
keys C-o
snap 03-reading "read 12 files"
# The cursor starts on the last row of the turn; up twice is the card, with
# the answer's prose between them.
keys k
keys k
snap 04-on-the-card "[enter] open it" "❯ ⚙ read 12 files"
keys Enter
# Opened in place: the reads come back as one group under the card, listed
# by name and counted past what the row holds, with the strip under them.
snap 05-opened "./ one.go · two.go" "❯   ▾ read 12 files" "… +6"
# The open card's group is a stop of its own: esc there folds the group to
# its head, the card staying open around it with its strip.
keys Escape
snap 06-refolded "[enter] unfold it" "❯   ▸ read 12 files"
# Esc is one level per press: the next closes the card the folded group
# starts, and only the one after leaves reading mode.
keys Escape
snap 06b-card-closed "[enter] open it" "❯ ⚙ read 12 files"
# The way out of reading mode, and the session's.
keys Escape
snap 07-prompt "[enter] send"
keys C-c
snap 08-quit-armed "again quits"
keys C-c
snap 09-exit "that is everything the screen was holding"
