# What a question's shape costs the screen
# (docs/interface/surfaces.md#the-question-card).
#
# What a golden cannot say and this can: that a yes-or-no landing in a real
# terminal leaves the rail in its columns, the transcript in its pane and the
# frame under the card with the vitals on it — and that the pick-one after it
# takes all three, because a list is what the width is for.
setup printf 'package agent\n' > loop.go && printf 'package agent\n' > round.go
snap 01-start "Some things worth doing first"
keys "fix the round limit" Enter
snap 02-read "Reading the round accounting first"
keys "go on then" Enter
# The yes-or-no. It arrives over an empty draft holding the keyboard, and the
# rule between it and the frame is what says so.
snap 03-ask "Should the migration be reversible?"
# The same screen, waited for by the rail's own heading: had the card taken
# the surface the way every other decision does, this snap would never come.
snap 04-rail "SPEND"
# And the frame is still under it, with the vitals that never drop.
snap 05-vitals "gated"
# The keys are the card's own, unchanged, and the answer lands as the row it
# always did.
keys y
snap 06-answered "on the card"
keys "and the store" Enter
# The pick-one, which needs the width for its descriptions and its short
# fields — so it takes the screen, and the rail, the frame and the reading
# behind it are what pays for it.
snap 07-list "in the checkout already"
keys C-Space
snap 08-held "the full answer"
keys Enter
snap 09-sent "Thank you, that settles both"
keys C-c
keys C-c
snap 10-exit "that is everything the screen was holding"
