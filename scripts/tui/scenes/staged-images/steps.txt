# Every chip on the staged rail leads with a handle of its kind and number
# (docs/interface/surfaces.md#the-input-frame). Two pictures that are both
# called clipboard.png and a text file are staged by path, one `/paste` each;
# the strip reads Image#1, Image#2 and File#1, so the two pictures are two
# words the paste verbs take rather than one name that reaches the first.
# Then the three are sent, and each keeps a row under the message that opens
# it (docs/interface/surfaces.md#a-staged-attachment). A click is the
# terminal's own SGR mouse report, sent as literal bytes: a press then a
# release in one cell, column and row counted from 1; Image#2's row is row 12
# at 110 columns and row 16 at 60, where the staging notices wrap.
#
# What the capture is for: at 04 the strip above the box carries all three
# chips, each handle ahead of its name, and the staging notice above it says
# `attached File#1 (notes.txt, …)`. At 05 `/paste drop clipboard.png` is
# refused naming both handles, and all three chips are still there. At 06 the
# sent message has a tray of three rows, `⟨▣ Image#1⟩`, `⟨▣ Image#2⟩` and
# `⟨¶ File#1⟩`, none printing a key. At 07 a click on Image#2's row has opened
# its card, titled `clipboard.png · sent with turn 1`, with `[esc] back to the
# draft`. At 10 reading mode's cursor stands on Image#2's row and the bar says
# `[enter] open it`; at 11 and again at 13 enter on that same row opens the
# card with `[esc] back to reading`. At 16 `/paste show Image#1` opens the first
# picture's card by name.
